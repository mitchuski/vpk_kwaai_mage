Reading the KwaaiNet map

The constellation shows every peer the map's crawler can see, three ways: where it is, what it serves, and where it sits on the sovereignty lattice. Everything on it comes from one public document. This page says what each part means, what it does not claim, and where the doors lead.

01one document

What it reads.

GET /api/v1/state: the same document every node's health monitor polls and kwaai-cli reads to choose a model. It carries every peer the observer identified over kad or found named in a DHT record, the per-model server rows, the top contributors and the bootstrap states. Nothing else is read, so nothing else is drawn.

Served locally, the page reads the seam live through a proxy and refreshes every 60 s, the crawl period. If the seam does not answer, the baked snapshot is shown and the status line says baked. ?src=data/some.json reviews any saved document; ?node=<peer id> opens on one peer.

02one dial

Three seatings: globe, chain, star.

Globe. A placed peer sits where geoip put it, lifted a little by throughput. A peer with no location orbits the outer ring rather than vanishing. Bootstraps are octahedra at the relay's own pin, derived from the circuit addresses. The model's blocks ring the globe, coloured by coverage.

Chain. Every peer lies on the block axis of the largest model: its bar is the range it announces, lanes stack so overlaps read, peers with no range sit on the top row. Chain threads join two serving peers whose ranges let one follow the other in an inference chain.

Star. The 64 vertices of the sovereignty lattice in three dimensions, placed exactly as the Swordsman's Key places them: six icosahedral directions, one per axis, and each vertex the sum of its held directions. The holographic boundary ∂M is drawn over them as the succ cycle plus the 32 half-turn chords, 96 edges. Each peer sits at its vertex; peers sharing a vertex step outward from it. The blocks ring the equator. It turns, and you can orbit it. Hover a vertex for its bits, its stratum and the peers standing there.

Where a peer sits on the star. Its vertex x is the six readings of section 05, OR-ed into six bits. The Swordsman's Key gives each bit a direction in space: six unit vectors along icosahedral axes, one per dimension — value, computation, connection, memory, delegation, protection in bit order. The position is the sum over the six bits of (bit − ½) times its direction, scaled so the farthest vertex sits at the star's radius: a held dimension pushes the point half a step along its axis, an unheld one pulls it half a step back. So V0 and V63 are antipodes; the Mage's antipode bnot is the point reflection through the centre; two vertices one flip apart are one fixed step apart along that axis; and the more dimensions a peer holds, the nearer it stands to V63's pole, which is what the stratum colouring shows. The boundary's 96 edges are drawn between those points. Peers sharing a vertex step outward from it in turn, the first at the true vertex. The blocks' equator and the bootstraps' poles are display conventions, not lattice facts. The embedding itself is one faithful picture of the 6-cube, the one the Star already uses, so a key's lit vertices and the peers' vertices are the same points.

The coverage bar at the bottom is one cell per block: green two or more serving, amber one, blue loading only, red a gap. Click a cell to isolate the peers serving it. The − at its corner folds it to one line.

04one peer sees the prompt

The seam.

A KwaaiNet chain tokenizes the prompt on the coordinator and ships raw token IDs to the first block server, the only peer holding the embedding table. Peers after it receive f16 hidden states. The privacy boundary of a chain is one peer, and that peer sees the whole prompt. F1 · OBSERVED

Colour by seam: serving block 0, receives every routed prompt as token IDs · serving later blocks, hidden states · dim, not in the chain. A gold ring marks a VPK Eve host, which today holds embeddings in the clear. F4 · VERIFIED + REPORTED

Chain selection in the client sorts on a behaviour score and gates on nothing; the credential ladder never touches peer selection. F3 · VERIFIED The findings, with their commands, are in vpk_kwaai_mage.

05six readings

The lattice reading.

Six yes/no readings of what the contract says about a peer give a 6-bit vertex, V0 to V63, on the sovereignty lattice. Protection is the high bit, value the low bit, the canon's bit values. The rules are a THESIS, stated so they can be argued with one at a time in the model tab.

axisbitthe peer is read as holding it when
🛡️ protection32it announces at least one trust attestation
🤝 delegation16it is the first block server, holding the delegated prompt
📜 memory8it offers VPK storage
🔗 connection4it is named by a DHT record
⚡ computation2it is serving blocks with announced throughput
💎 value1it is among the top contributors

From the vertex the model's own machinery applies. Moves between two peers are named: succ 😊, neg ⚔️ the Swordsman's reflection, bnot 🧙 the Mage's antipode, a single flip, or a jump. A walk's T(π) = 1 + β Σ f(e)·g(nₑ), with f = 0.25·flips + 0.75·|Δstratum| and g = 1/√(times walked), β = 1. The network term (1 + Σ wᵢnᵢ/N₀)^k weights each stratum by Pascal's row, 1 · 6 · 15 · 20 · 15 · 6 · 1, with N₀ the peer count and k on a slider. Betweenness on the chain graph names the peers the most shortest paths cross.

All of these are display registers. β, k and the edge weights are uncalibrated by the model's own account; the page shows the numbers so the model can be looked at, not to settle anything.

06a reading you chose

The key.

Walk peers with shift+click, or ✦ walk it on a card. The walk is a thread through them and T(π) accumulates. The four palette wells are the City Key carrier palette: cool is the ocean, warm the land and labels, sword the boundary reading, mage the threads. They are kept locally and travel in your key.

Export writes a version-1 City Key: a name, the palette, the lattice vertices your walked peers sit on as its lit set, the walked peers and moves under source, a witness if you observed, and κ, the SHA-256 of the canonical form with only kappa excluded, so any instrument can re-derive and check it. The keepsake PNG carries the same key in a cityKey text chunk, so it opens in the Star at /lattice, /star and /skye.

Import a key, JSON or keepsake PNG, and it arrives as its own layer in its own palette: the manifold around the globe, the dual cores at the centre, the 64 inside with ∂M, its lit vertices burning. The globe keeps its colours. Peers standing on a lit vertex are ringed in the key's sword colour; fold to the star to see them on the ring. κ is re-derived and checked on import.

The whole network as a star key seats every peer at its vertex and lists the edges the network realises on ∂M as vertex pairs. Seating, palette and names stay out of its packets.root, so the same crawl reads the same root on the globe, the chain, the star, or in another instrument: the boundary encodes the bulk. Name your own node in the key tab and it is ringed and named as self.

A key is a reading you chose. It is not a credential, not a proof, and not standing in anything.

07presence

Observing.

▶ observe runs a light around your walk, or around the serving chain when nothing is walked, and counts each visit into a witness by vertex, the same shape the Star's lattice mints when a key's focus is walked. ● rec on the coverage bar writes one row of bits per crawl, a strip of what the network looked like while you watched. Both ride in an exported key. Both are records of attention that a later ceremony could attest; neither is a proof, and the page says so.

0996 over 64

The boundary encodes the bulk.

The lattice has 64 vertices. The Swordsman's Key draws its boundary ∂M with 96 edges: the succ cycle, 64 of them, and the 32 half-turn chords from each vertex to the one across the ring. The model reads the ratio, 96/64 = 1.5, as the holographic principle in discrete form: the surface carries the interior. That is what a zero-knowledge proof does too, and it is why the map's boundary key is built the way it is.

The boundary key writes down two things only: which vertices are occupied, with how many peers, and which vertex pairs the network realises as edges, chain threads and one-flip pairs. Its root is the κ of that pair of lists. Seating, palette, names, highlights: none of it is in the root. So a peer on the globe and the same peer on the star are the same vertex, and the same crawl seen by two people in two palettes reads the same root. The network's shape, not its picture, is what the key names.

The claim behind this, that value can be computed from boundary observation alone, is the model's C9 and it is a conjecture. The map does not test it. It only takes care that what it hashes is a boundary and nothing else.

10the star as a trust picture

Every node at its vertex.

Fold to the star and the network is no longer a map of where machines are. It is a picture of what each one holds, on the same 64 vertices a City Key lights. A key's lit set and a crawl's occupied vertices are subsets of one ring, so they can be laid on top of each other: import a key and the peers standing on its lit vertices are ringed in its own sword colour, on the globe and on the ring alike. That is the beginning of a trust presentation: not a score, but a shared surface on which a key and a network can be compared for overlap, the way the Star's sky compares two keys.

The vertex a peer sits on is a reading of the contract, six rules that are written down in the model tab and in section 05 and can be argued with one at a time. Change a rule and the peers move; the key you export afterwards carries a different κ, as it should. The reading is the arguable part; the ring, the operators, the strata and the κ rule are the model's own.

The working record of how this took shape, and what is still open, is the lane's sibling lane's companion chronicle, chronicles/2026-09-27_the-map-as-constellation.md in this repository.

11the honest boundary

What it is not.

  • Not a measurement. A dashboard is a reading surface; the tier, the seam colouring and the lattice reading render claims so they can be felt, they do not establish them.
  • Not history. The contract carries no first-seen, last-seen or throughput history, so the card says so instead of drawing a sparkline.
  • Not an endorsement graph. No endorsement edges exist in the document yet; none are invented.
  • Not calibrated. β, k and the edge weights are display registers; the model itself lists them as open.
  • Not a proof. A key is a reading you chose; a witness is a count of attention.
